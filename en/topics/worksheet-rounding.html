Rounding: practice notes

Rounding gives an estimate at a chosen place, so first identify that place. To round 347 to the nearest ten, look at the ones digit and decide which ten is closer; to round to the nearest hundred, compare the distance to 300 and 400. A number line makes the midpoint visible. For decimals, keep the requested place in view and inspect the digit immediately to its right. The retained digit changes only when the next digit meets the rounding rule; digits farther right do not determine the decision by themselves.

Use estimation to check whether a calculation is reasonable, not as a substitute when an exact answer is required. Before rounding, underline the target place and circle the deciding digit. Then rewrite the rounded number and explain the direction of change. A common error is to round the wrong column or to drop all trailing place markers, making the estimate?s scale unclear. In multi-step problems, decide whether to round each measurement or only the final result, since early rounding can change later calculations.

Begin with numbers near a midpoint so the learner can compare both possible rounded values. Then practise values clearly above or below the midpoint and move to decimal places. Ask for a quick estimate before a worksheet calculation and compare it with the exact result afterward. If the estimate is far away, check the target place and the deciding digit. The rounding pages offer different place values and number formats; choose a set that matches the classroom goal and keep the reason for rounding visible throughout the work.
