Kindergarten: join small groups

Young learners understand addition best when they can see two groups become one larger group. Picture addition gives each number a visible meaning. Number lines and ten frames help children move from counting every object toward recognizing parts of a whole. Keep the numbers small and invite the child to draw, point or count aloud before writing an equation.

Grade 1: addition facts within 20

First grade practice grows from making 10 to adding within 20. Mix direct equations with missing-number and word-problem formats so children learn to recognize the same addition relationship in different forms. A child who still counts every addend may benefit from a number line; a child who knows the facts can use a short mixed page for fluency.

Grades 2–4: place value and regrouping

Multi-digit addition depends on lining up ones, tens and hundreds. Begin without regrouping so the written layout is familiar, then add one regrouping step at a time. Ask learners to estimate first and check whether the final sum is reasonable. Three-digit pages provide more place-value practice after two-digit regrouping is reliable.
