Review first, then try regrouping

Start with a no-regrouping sheet to check alignment of ones and tens. When regrouping is needed, ask the child to explain what changes in each column. If facts within 20 are still difficult, give those a short review before a longer calculation.

Connect numbers to everyday quantities

The coin activity uses pennies, nickels, dimes and quarters, with answers in cents. Length activities compare labeled measurements in centimeters; they are not ruler-to-scale tests or U.S. unit conversion drills. Place-value activities focus on hundreds, tens and ones.

  • Time to five minutes
  • Addition and subtraction word problems

Explore equal groups before multiplication facts

Use pictures and repeated addition to discuss equal groups and arrays. These activities introduce multiplication ideas; they do not require second graders to memorize an extended times table. Children ready for a next step can explore third grade practice.
