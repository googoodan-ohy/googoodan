Kindergarten: count, match and make numbers

Kindergarten number sense starts with one-to-one counting: one count word for each object. Count-and-color and matching pages let children point, touch or mark each item. Ten frames show how a quantity relates to five and ten, which prepares children to see small groups without recounting every object. Use one short page at a time and ask the child to say how the picture proves the answer.

Grade 1: compare, order and find missing numbers

First graders connect counting to written number relationships. Comparing pages build the language of greater than, less than and equal to. Number order and missing-number paths help children notice the sequence instead of guessing. Number bonds show that a whole can be split and recombined, supporting addition and subtraction within 20.

Grade 2: understand place value to 1,000

Place value explains why the same digit can represent ones, tens or hundreds. Build a number with base-ten blocks first, then move to standard, word and expanded forms. Comparing three-digit numbers is easier when children check the hundreds place before the tens and ones. These printable choices keep the representation visible while the numbers change from one set to the next.
