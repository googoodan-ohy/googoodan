Kindergarten: take away from a small group

For an early subtraction lesson, begin with a group the child can see. Cross out or move away some objects, then count what remains. Pictures make the action clear before the minus sign is introduced. Number-line pages later show subtraction as moving back from a starting number. Keep the values within five until the child can explain both the starting amount and the amount taken away.

Grade 1: subtraction facts within 20

First graders connect subtraction to addition and use known facts to find a difference. Practice within 10 before moving to teen numbers. Missing-number questions encourage children to identify the unknown rather than rely on one memorized format. Word problems add meaning by asking whether a situation describes taking away, comparing or finding a missing part.

Grades 2–4: subtract with place value

Before regrouping, check that a learner can line up ones, tens and hundreds. Two-digit subtraction without regrouping isolates place-value structure. When regrouping is introduced, use base-ten language: one ten becomes ten ones, or one hundred becomes ten tens. Estimating the difference and checking with addition are useful habits for larger numbers.
