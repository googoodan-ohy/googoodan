Choosing fraction and decimal worksheets

Fractions and decimals describe parts of a whole in different forms. Choose a worksheet by the representation it uses: shaded parts, a number line, equivalent fractions, decimal notation, or a calculation. A picture page can make the size of a part visible; a number line helps compare values; a calculation sheet requires the learner to keep the parts or place values aligned. Look at one example before printing so the worksheet matches the idea being reviewed rather than introducing several unfamiliar steps at once.

Move between a fraction and a decimal

Select Worksheet to attempt the questions, Answer Key to check, or both for guided practice. Use scrap paper to draw a model for one question, then see whether the remaining questions use the same idea. New Numbers gives another set in the same format. For tenths and hundredths, a place-value chart can help the learner name what each digit means. For fractions, label the whole and make equal parts before comparing how many parts are selected. For a hard copy, select Letter paper in the print dialog.

Try a quarter and hundredths example

One quarter is the same as 25 hundredths, or 0.25. To see why, divide a whole into four equal parts and then into one hundred equal parts; one of the four large parts covers 25 of the hundredths. Compare 0.25 with 0.3 by writing 0.3 as 0.30. Since 30 hundredths is greater than 25 hundredths, 0.3 is larger. The extra zero does not change a decimal's value; it makes the columns easier to compare.

Common errors to notice

Do not add denominators when adding fractions with equal-sized parts; first check whether the pieces are the same size. A fraction such as 3/8 is not larger than 1/2 just because 8 exceeds 2. Compare the parts using a picture, common denominator or number line. In decimal work, align the decimal points and compare equal place values, not just the first digits. If the answer seems surprising, shade the fraction or estimate its location between zero and one before checking the answer key.
