Why Kids Should Not Add the Bottom Numbers in Fractions

When children add fractions, the most common mistake is adding both the top and bottom numbers. A picture makes the reason clear.

Leo sees 2/8 + 3/8 and writes 5/16. He has added every number he can see. Shading pieces of the same-sized bar shows that the eighths have not changed; only the number of shaded pieces has grown.

Keep the size of the pieces

Draw one bar split into eight equal pieces. Shade two, then three more. Count five shaded eighths. The denominator tells the size of each piece, so it stays eight.

Use models before shortcuts

Once the picture is clear, let the child write the number sentence. A worksheet with fresh models helps the idea stick.
