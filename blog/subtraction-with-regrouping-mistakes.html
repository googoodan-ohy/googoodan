How to Help a Child With 2 Digit Subtraction With Regrouping

Regrouping in subtraction is hard when a child sees it as crossing out a digit instead of exchanging one ten for ten ones.

Noah looks at 42 − 18 and says, “I cannot take 8 from 2.” That is a useful moment, not a failure. He needs to see one ten become ten ones before he borrows a mark from the tens column.

Trade, do not just borrow

Build 42 with four tens and two ones. Trade one ten for ten ones, then count twelve ones. The written regrouping mark now matches something the child can explain.

Check with addition

After solving, add the answer to the number taken away. This makes subtraction a relationship rather than a rule to memorize.
