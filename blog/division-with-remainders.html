When a Child Meets Division With Remainders

A remainder makes sense when a child can see the amount that does not fit into an equal group.

Ava shares 14 stickers among four friends. She gives each friend three, then notices two stickers are still on the table. Those two are not an error—they are the remainder.

Build equal groups first

Use counters, draw circles, or make small groups on paper. Ask how many go in every group and what is left. Write the remainder only after the child has named the leftover amount.

Use one quick check

Multiply the quotient by the divisor and add the remainder. If the result returns to the starting number, the answer is consistent.
