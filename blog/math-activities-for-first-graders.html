Three Math Activities for First Graders That Use Paper and Pencils

First grade practice works best in short bursts. A page that asks a child to count, circle, match, or draw can feel more like a task to solve than another row of facts to finish.

On a rainy afternoon, a first grader finishes a page of addition facts and slides the pencil away. Instead of asking for another row of numbers, put a ten frame and a few counters on the table. A five-minute activity gives the child something to touch, notice, and explain—and often brings the pencil back out naturally.

1. Count, point, and compare

Start with two small picture groups. Ask the child to point while counting each group, then say which has more, fewer, or the same number. Have them explain how they know before writing a comparison sign. This helps connect number words, quantities, and symbols.

Open count and circle worksheets for a printable starting point.

2. Build ten with a ten frame

Ten frames make number partners visible. Give a child a crayon and ask them to color the empty spaces needed to make ten. Then ask a related question: “If there are 7 dots, how many spaces are empty?” The child can see 7 + 3 = 10 instead of trying to remember it as an isolated fact.

Open ten frame and make-ten activities.

3. Turn addition into a short picture story

Use two groups of simple objects: stickers, blocks, fruit, or drawn circles. Say, “There are 6 apples on the table and 4 more are added. How many are there now?” Let the child draw or cross out objects before writing the number sentence. For subtraction, begin with one group and physically cross out the part that leaves.

Keep the ending positive

Stop while the child still has energy. One page does not need to be finished to count as useful practice. Save the last question for tomorrow, or use it as a quick check before starting a new page. Repeating a familiar activity with new numbers gives children a chance to feel capable while their number sense grows.
