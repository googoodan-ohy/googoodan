Times tables practice · Charts

How to use this page

This page is for primary pupils, parents and teachers who want to practise the times tables. Choose the tables, then print the practice sheet and the answer key, each on one A4 page.

New numbers shuffles the questions, so the same table can be practised again and again. To save a PDF, choose Save as PDF in the print window.

Tips for learning the tables

  • Learn one table at a time, a few minutes every day.
  • The order does not change the answer: 3 × 7 = 7 × 3.
  • The 4 times table is double the 2 times table; for 9, subtract the number once from the 10 times table.
  • Check with the chart, then practise on the sheet with the answers hidden.

Multiplication examples

  • 3 × 7 = 21: three groups of 7 make 21.
  • 9 × 6 = 60 − 6 = 54: take one 6 away from the 10 times table.
  • 0 × 8 = 0: zero groups of 8 make zero.

Common mistakes

  • Multiplying is not adding the two numbers: 3 × 7 is 21, not 10.
  • Every answer in the 0 times table is 0; in the 1 times table, the answer stays the same.
  • For the 9 times table, the digits in an answer add to a multiple of 9: 5 + 4 = 9 in 54 and 9 + 9 = 18 in 99.

Practise times tables with patterns and checks

Use this page to choose a table, consult the chart and practise with a printable sheet. Begin with one table whose facts are partly familiar, rather than starting with a mixed set. The chart can help a learner locate a product, but the practice sheet is useful for recall without looking. Short repeats give time to notice patterns and correct a missed fact. The New Numbers control supplies another arrangement, so the same table can be practised again without memorising a fixed question order.

Use related facts

For 6 × 7, start with the easier 5 × 7 = 35 and add one more group of 7 to get 42. For 9 × 6, use ten groups of 6 and subtract one group: 60 − 6 = 54. The order of factors can switch without changing the product, so 3 × 7 and 7 × 3 both equal 21. These strategies connect facts instead of treating every multiplication as isolated memory.

Choose the table or mixed practice that fits the session. Select Worksheet for questions, Answer Key for checking, or both if the pages will be used together. Review the print preview and keep the answer page aside during the attempt. For a digital copy, select Save as PDF in the browser’s print dialog. If the sheet contains more questions than can be completed comfortably, finish a section and return to the remaining items later.

Catch common errors

Multiplication is repeated groups, not the sum of the two factors: 3 × 7 is 21, not 10. In the zero table every product is zero; in the one table the product stays equal to the other factor. When recalling a difficult fact, say the related fact aloud and calculate it, then check the result on the chart. Avoid changing a correct answer because another nearby product looks familiar.

After a sheet, circle facts that took extra time and practise those again. A quick check with the answer page confirms the result; explaining one strategy confirms how it was found. Move to mixed tables after single-table practice is steady. If a fact is still uncertain, return to its table or chart rather than guessing from a pattern that does not apply to every product.
